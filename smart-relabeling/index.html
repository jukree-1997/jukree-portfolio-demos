How the review and auto-labeling loop works

Bottles and cars illustrate initial suggestions from a pretrained COCO class. Spare parts represent a custom class: label an example first. After human edits, the original app can fine-tune and update only unedited images. Reviewed and edited images are protected. Repeated runs can reuse a previous fine-tuned model if the human labels have not changed. All AI results here are scripted illustrations, not real inference.

BEHIND THE PROJECT / MAR–MAY 2026

Built around the work.
And the people doing it.

An internal tool at Baksters Co., Ltd., created to help staff prepare detection datasets and customer demo models with less repetitive labeling.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Labeling was the bottleneck.

Preparing object-detection datasets took substantial manual effort. Customer data could also be private, making a local desktop workflow important.

02 / MY CONTRIBUTION

One owner, end to end.

I initiated the tool and independently designed and built the UI, architecture, and application logic, then refined the workflow to make it easier for colleagues to use.

03 / THE OUTCOME

More time for the model.

The team reported labeling 8,000 images in under two days, supporting faster model preparation. This is a team-reported outcome, without a controlled before/after time benchmark.

ARCHITECTURE / ORIGINAL APPLICATION

A local pipeline with a human checkpoint.

The interface coordinates services through an event bus. Dataset, configuration, and model repositories keep storage concerns separate from labeling and training.
